# Sarcomere assembly handoff — 2026-09-15

Supersedes `HANDOFF_2026-08-03.md`. Covers the minimal-wrap-rig / motor-duty
work of 2026-09-14 and 2026-09-15.

## Executive summary

The central question — *does having several myosin heads bound to the same
actin align that actin to the myosin with **no** alignment potential* — is
answered **yes**, but only by the confined tilted rig (job `43436344`): actins
started at 60° and 85° rotate to 4–7° in **12/12 seeds** at
`am_alignment_k = 0`, ordered by `<kappa> = P2 * k_rho * dz^2 / 2` with a
threshold near 1 pN·µm/rad. That result stands and is the load-bearing one.

The minimal **wrap** rig is a different matter and consumed this session. Its
motors barely engage: duty per head slot **0.49%** against production's
**24.83%**, with 95.9% of myosin-frames carrying no bound head at all. This is
not the motor model — every binding parameter is identical between rig and
production and `am_k_weak_off` is 4× *slower* in the rig. It is stoichiometry:
`minimal_catch_balanced` gives each myosin half one designated actin and puts 4
actins in a 21 µm³ box (0.19/µm³ against production's 66.67), so once the single
partner drifts out of reach there is nothing else in the cell to bind.

Raising motor engagement works but breaks the Z-disc. Arm `bothx`
(`am_k_powerstroke` 200→1000, `am_k_strong_off` 160→40, `am_k_on` 100→400)
reaches duty **21.5%** — essentially production — but the working motors win the
tug-of-war the rig is built to stage, dragging the central actin pair 1.8 µm
apart and destroying every crosslink. Crucially, **`am_k_on` carries the whole
effect**: the two dwell knobs alone reach only 1.59%.

Two attempts to stop the actin escaping both failed, one of them because the
mechanism is dead code. The live hypothesis, not yet resolved, is that the
actins are simply diffusing too fast — `actin_crowding_perpendicular = 10` is
the outlier in a file that already assumes 160 for tumbling.

### ACTN2 status-2 retention — the defect is real, at production scale

`lifetime = aa_base_lifetime + [status==2]*aa_directional_base_lifetime + aa_lifetime_coefficient * effective_load = 0.1 + 0.05 + 0.4 F`

| | wrap rig ctl (5 s) | production (1 s) | production (5 s) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| promotions 1→2 | 5 | 7 270 | **84 268** |
| `effective_load` @ promotion (median) | 0.365 | 1.495 | 1.847 pN |
| lifetime granted @ promotion (median) | 0.296 | 0.748 | **0.889 s** |
| detachments 2→0 | 5 | 5 775 | 80 549 |
| `effective_load` @ detachment (median) | **0.000** | **0.000** | **0.000** |
| lifetime @ detachment (median) | **0.150** | **0.150** | **0.150 s** |
| `single_link_tension` @ detachment (median) | 1.81 | 2.88 | 2.42 pN |
| status-2 links, final frame | 0 | 1 495 | **3 719** |
| demotions 2→1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

`aa_status2_locked_until_detach = 1` and `aa_status2_unlock_rate = 0` in
production, so **the lock preserves the status but not the lifetime** — the
off-rate is still recomputed from the instantaneous load each step.

**Production tolerates it by throughput, not longevity**: 84 268 promotions
outrun 80 549 detachments, so the population climbs monotonically 0 → 2 046 →
3 719 and is still climbing at 5 s. Each link lives 76 ms instead of the 889 ms
granted, but there are 1600 actins and 467 123 fresh associations to replace it.

Detachment reasons for the 80 549 status-2 exits: stochastic 72.9%,
geometric_contour 14.0%, am_overlap_occlusion 12.0%, collision 1.1%. Only the
stochastic share is lifetime-governed, capping any retention fix at ~2.6×.

**The EMA fix failed** (job `43467880`): `effective_load` at detachment moved
0.0000 → 0.0044 (10 ms window) → 0.0160 pN (60 ms) of the 1.06 pN needed,
because support is zero in 100% of link-frames to 1 d.p. (mean 0.00023 pN, only
0.02% above the 0.25 pN threshold) and an EMA converges to the mean. Remedy is
peak-hold with exponential decay, **not yet built**.

**But it partly self-corrects at high duty.** At `bothx` the support signal goes
from 0.02% of link-frames above threshold to **49.08%** (mean 0.0002 → 2.35 pN),
and lifetime at 2→0 detachment rises 0.150 → **0.384 s** with no code change.

### Escape mechanics

- The actin sits at the relaxed **77 nm** spacing until the last head releases,
  then crosses the 130 nm capture limit in **5.8 ms mean / 5.0 ms median**
  (frame-limited; 5 ms save interval). The **~34 ms** figure used in earlier
  handoffs and notes is **wrong by ~6×**.
- Gaps that stay inside 130 nm always heal (36/36). Gaps that cross it mostly do
  not (96% of the 25 long ones crossed).
- OFF/SRX is not implicated: 5.70 of 36 heads during gaps against a 4.95
  run-wide mean; in ctl it is *lower* during gaps.
- Per-actin, not OR'd across the cell: in `bothx` actin 0 is unbound in **89.0%**
  of frames with an 1810 ms gap; actins 1/2/3 are 58.0 / 41.2 / 19.8%. The whole
  cell also goes fully detached once, for **240 ms** at t = 730–785 ms, and
  recovers.
- Engagement is winner-take-all per thick filament: myosin 0 favours actin 2
  over actin 0 by **12:1** (2.47 vs 0.21 heads) despite the cell being built with
  inversion symmetry. The initializer's `balanced_seeds` pairing only holds at
  t = 0; once the actins tumble the polarity-correct half changes.

### Confinement sweep — job `43602794`, 5 arms × 3 seeds, all COMPLETED

**`am_tether_occupancy_floor` is dead code in the regime it was written for.**
The floor at `sarcomere.cpp:6775` sits inside `if (am_bonds[i][j] != 1) continue;`
(line 6600), and `am_bonds == 1` means the pair already has ≥1 **bound** head
(set at 2028, cleared by `_drop_unseen_am_bonds`, restored at 11969 only for
weak/strong). So it cannot fire during a zero-head gap — exactly the case its own
comment claims to handle:

> "A constitutive share of the radial containment, independent of whether any
> head is currently attached. The occupancy gate above is keyed to bound heads
> and therefore vanishes exactly when they let go, which is when an
> unconstrained pair drifts apart."

Independently, `radial_occupancy = max(bound_head_count/am_tether_saturation_heads, floor)`
is already ≥ 1/6 = **0.1667** whenever the loop does run, so any floor below that
is a no-op regardless. Both arms tried (0.05, 0.15) were below it. Confirmed:
`flr05`/`flr15` reproduce `base` to two decimals.

Slowing diffusion 2× and 4× made things **worse**:

| arm | D_perp | T_bound | T_gap | % gaps escaping | fraction of time bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| base | 0.1855 | 97.8 ms | **159.6 ms** | **56.2%** | 37.3% |
| crw20 | 0.0927 | 58.5 ms | **220.9 ms** | 54.6% | 20.6% |
| crw40 | 0.0464 | 69.3 ms | **244.9 ms** | 48.2% | 21.4% |

The reason is an asymmetry: `T_bound` is set by head kinetics and is
D-independent, while the *return journey* after an escape is pure diffusion over
a fixed distance and scales as 1/D. And the escape-vs-recapture **race** is
D-invariant, because recapture is also diffusion-limited — which is what the
near-flat escaping fraction shows. So lowering D buys almost nothing on the race
and pays fully on the return.

### Crowding bracket — job `43607829`, IN FLIGHT at time of writing

Hypothesis (from the user, and it is the live one): the actins are simply
diffusing too fast, so escape is an artifact to remove rather than a race to win.
`t_escape = x^2/(2 D_perp)` with x = 50 nm:

| `crowding_perp` | 10 | 40 | 100 | 400 | 1000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| D_perp (µm²/s) | 0.1855 | 0.0464 | 0.0185 | 0.0046 | 0.0019 |
| t_escape | 6.7 ms | 27 ms | 67 ms | 269 ms | 674 ms |

Measured gaps are median 15 ms / mean 160 ms, so escape should stop mattering
between 100 and 400. **Job `43602794` only reached 40, which the table shows is
still escape-dominated — that test was underpowered, not a refutation.** Arms:
`c10 c100 c400 c1000 c400par` (the last also raises
`actin_crowding_parallel` to 400, to separate radial caging from immobilising
the rod). Analyzer staged at `/tmp/crowdanalyse.py`.

Supporting argument: `actin_crowding_perpendicular = 10` is the outlier inside
the rig's own parameter set, which already uses `actin_crowding_tumble = 160`
for the same cytoplasm.

## Corrections to earlier conclusions

- **Escape time is ~5.8 ms, not ~34 ms.** Everything reasoned from the 34 ms
  figure (including the "titin would patch an artifact" argument) needs
  rechecking.
- **The wrap rig's initial A–M separation is 80.0 nm exactly**
  (`myosin.radius + actin_radius + am_surface_gap`, `initialization.cpp:1340`),
  *not* 152.6 nm. The earlier figure came from a parallel-rod degenerate branch
  in an analysis routine, not from the model.
- **Capture is gated on excess extension, not centreline distance.**
  `head_reach_surface_distance <= am_cutoff`, where that quantity is zero at the
  preferred spacing. So capture reaches ~**130 nm** of centreline and rupture
  ~**180 nm** — not the 80 nm implied by `actin_radius + am_surface_gap + am_cutoff`.
- **"A bound head in 98.1% of frames" is not stability.** That was an OR over 4
  actins and 2 myosins. Per-actin occupancy is the metric; report it.
- **`am_tether_occupancy_floor` is not a usable knob** without a code change
  (see above). Earlier notes recommending it — including the claim that
  `floor = 0` is "the culprit" for production escape — assumed a mechanism the
  control flow does not deliver.
- **The bound-actin alignment correlation is not evidence for the mechanism.**
  In `bothx`, bound actin-frames sit at median θ = 7.7° while unbound ones are
  isotropic (56.9° vs 60°) — but ctl, with **1.50** heads, is *better* aligned
  (9.2°, 93% under 20°) than `bothx` with **3.73** heads (16.3°, 74%). The
  inversion points to the association gate *selecting* aligned actins rather than
  binding aligning them. The causal evidence remains the tilted rig, whose
  design (start at 60°/85°, watch rotation) selection cannot explain.
- **Outcomes are seed-determined, not OpenMP-determined.** The confinement
  campaign accidentally ran 3 seeds × 3 physics-replicates; replicates agree
  closely even though trajectories decorrelate from frame 1 (positions differ by
  up to 5.3 µm, the box size). Resolving arms needs more **seeds**, not more
  replicates.

## Open questions

1. Does raising `actin_crowding_perpendicular` to 100–1000 stop escape outright?
   Job `43607829` answers this. If yes, the whole escape/recapture framing was
   an artifact of an under-crowded thin filament.
2. `am_k_on = 400 /s` carries the entire `bothx` result and is the least
   defensible parameter in it. Is it justifiable, or is it compensating for the
   crowding error in (1)?
3. Why is actin 0 starved 12:1 by its sibling on the same thick filament?
   `am_max_heads_per_actin = 24` is not binding at 3.3 heads, so if a cap is
   doing this it is per-site ownership, not per-actin.
4. Can the Z-disc survive once the motors work? `aa_base_lifetime = 0.1 s`
   against ~2.5 s in the α-actinin literature is the largest single discrepancy
   left in the model, and `bothx` now puts real load on those links.
5. Unexplained: 14 in-range frames after t = 440 ms in the s2retention ctl run
   produce zero binding, six of them at near-ideal geometry (72–114 nm,
   θ = 2.7–10.1°, 320 nm axial window, activation 1.0, 26–28 heads free).
6. Unexplained: one of 61 gap episodes reports the actin at 738 nm on its last
   *bound* frame, which should be impossible past ~180 nm. Either
   `am_head_bound_actin_current` lags the state array by a frame or the distance
   is wrong for that case.

## Operational notes

- The wrap rig costs **~6 min per task** on 4 CPUs; a 5-arm × 3-seed campaign is
  ~15 min wall clock. There is no reason to run it at n=3 given the seed-driven
  bimodality — use ≥12 seeds.
- `am_cutoff`, `am_optimal`, `max_actin_displacement`, `max_myosin_displacement`
  are **still not persisted** to HDF5; read them from the campaign sbatch.
  `am_tether_occupancy_floor` and the crowding factors *are* persisted.
- Always verify swept parameters against the HDF5 `meta` attrs before trusting a
  campaign. Params live on the **`meta` group**, not the file root.
- Write analysis scripts locally and `scp` them; nested heredocs and nested
  `ssh` inside a command both mangled scripts repeatedly this session.
- Verify patches at **1 thread** and compare **bytes**, not `np.array_equal`
  (which is False on NaN and produced a false alarm).
