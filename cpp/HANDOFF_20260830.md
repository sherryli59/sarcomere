## 2. THE WALL-TIME TRAP (most likely thing to bite next)

`owners` caps wall time at **2 days**. Completion times observed so far are
**27-35 h on fast nodes**, but the same work on slow `owners` nodes runs
**55-80 h** and cannot finish inside 48 h.

On 2026-08-30 tasks 2, 3 and 7 had run 44 h and stood at 72%, 90% and 55% with
4 h left. All three would have hit TIMEOUT. **A TIMEOUT breaks the aggregate's
`afterok:41142129` dependency, which would silently cancel `41142130` after the
whole campaign finished.**

They were rescued with:

```bash
ssh sherlock 'scontrol requeue 41142129_2 41142129_3 41142129_7'
```

Requeue is safe: `SLURM_RESTART_COUNT>=1` makes the script's resume branch fire,
so the run continues from its last 500-step checkpoint with a fresh 48 h.
Verified end-to-end (section 4).

**Standing action for whoever picks this up:** poll for tasks approaching the
wall and requeue them before they expire.

```bash
# tasks with under 6 h left
ssh sherlock 'squeue -j 41142129 -h -o "%i %L %M" -a | awk "\$2 ~ /^[0-5]:/"'
# per-task progress
ssh sherlock 'for f in /scratch/users/xsli/sarcomere/assembly_controls_A1600_M320_Lx6_20260828/data/*.h5; do
python3 -c "
import h5py,sys,numpy as np
h=h5py.File(sys.argv[1],\"r\",locking=False); n=int(h[\"meta/committed_frames\"][0])
s=np.asarray(h[\"state/current_step\"][:n]).ravel()
print(\"%-46s %7d/500000 %5.1f%%\" % (sys.argv[1].split(\"/\")[-1], s[-1], 100*s[-1]/500000))" "$f"; done'
```

`turn0p35_seed20260815` (task 7) was running at ~6.3k steps/h, i.e. ~80 h total.
It will need **two** 48-h cycles even after the requeue. If repeated requeueing
gets tedious, move the stragglers to `rotskoff` only, which allows 7 days:

```bash
ssh sherlock 'scontrol update jobid=41142129_7 partition=rotskoff timelimit=3-00:00:00'
```
(only works while the task is PENDING; you cannot change the partition of a
running job.)

## 3. Scientific findings

### Turnover is a clean null

Four levels (`k_turn` = 0, 0.1, 0.35, 1.0 s^-1), 12 seeds, Lx=3: `lcc` spans
0.082-0.192 with total overlap between arms. No effect on any assembly
observable.

The mechanism is verifiably correct, so this is a real negative and not a
plumbing failure: campaign-wide audit gave **2773 renewal events placed, 0
placement failures, all geometry finite**, with per-arm Poisson z <= 0.94
(66 vs 64 expected at k=0.1; 238 vs 224 at k=0.35).

### Motor availability is the lever

Lx=3, all arms otherwise identical:

| arm | p_ON | lcc per seed | mean | status-2 links |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| turn0-turn1 (full ON) | 1.00 | 0.082 - 0.192 (12 seeds) | 0.10-0.14 | 193-282 |
| healthy | 0.12 | 0.291 0.309 0.327 | 0.309 | 2 |
| intermediate | 0.37 | 0.231 0.234 0.495 | 0.320 | 36 |
| hcm | 0.61 | 0.121 0.210 0.301 | 0.211 | 142 |

Robust: **all 12 full-ON seeds <= 0.192, 8 of 9 reduced-availability seeds
>= 0.21.** Cutting availability roughly triples connectivity.

Not robust: any finer ordering. Intermediate and HCM each span most of the
dynamic range on their own.

**Connectivity and maturation are anti-correlated.** status-2 links go
2 -> 36 -> 142 -> ~240 as availability rises, exactly opposite to `lcc`. You
currently cannot have both.

**Mechanism (from the snapshots).** Myosin nematic order along x is monotonic in
availability: S = 0.24 (healthy) -> 0.64 (intermediate) -> 0.86 (full ON) ->
0.92 (HCM). Motor engagement is what holds thick filaments parallel. So there
are two opposing effects: more motors align the scaffold, and more motors drag
actins faster than ACTN2 can crosslink. Healthy is not limited by tearing, it is
limited by the scaffold tumbling.

Note tension p90 is **0.00 in every reduced-availability arm** and 3.8-4.3 pN in
the full-ON arms. The sensor works; the motor arms genuinely carry no load, so
thick-filament mechanosensitive recruitment never engages and those arms sit at
their basal ON fraction. "Healthy-like" is therefore *fixed* low availability,
not *feedback-regulated* low availability.

### Lx=3 was the wrong box — this invalidated the axial readouts

The model selects a **2.00 um sarcomere period**. In Lx=6 the myosin band
spectrum is sharply peaked at harmonic **k=3** (0.842 and 0.590 in the two
finished turn0 seeds at 5 s). A 2.00 um period in Lx=3 needs k=1.5, which PBC
cannot host, so the Lx=3 spectrum is flat (max 0.166 at k=1).

Confirmed by the time series: Lx=3 M order **rises to ~0.30 by 0.5 s then decays
to 0.27 by 1 s**, and Z order peaks ~0.22 at 0.4 s then falls to 0.17. Order
forms and then breaks down — the signature of frustration, not slow convergence.
Lx=6 climbs monotonically to M 0.71 / Z 0.60 at 5 s.

Lx=6 turn0 (n=2) vs Lx=3 turn0 (n=3):

| | Lx=3 @1 s | Lx=6 @5 s |
|---|---:|---:|
| M order | 0.267 | 0.694 |
| Z order | 0.175 | 0.580 |
| lcc | 0.119 | 0.196 |
| status-2 lcc | 0.016 | **0.155** |
| status-2 links | 212 | **3921** |

The mature network is the headline: 18x more status-2 links, 10x larger mature
component. Axial banding lets barbed ends register into planes.

**Lx=6 is now a standing instruction from Sherry.** At the campaign density
(66.7 actin/um^3, 13.3 myosin/um^3) that means A1600/M320. Do not propose a
shorter axial box to save compute; density matching does not rescue it, because
the constraint is commensurability.

### Percolation is blocked by packing, not link range or redundancy

`P_perp_both` is **zero in every run measured** — three box sizes, two link
stoichiometries, out to 5 s, including Lx=6 with proper banding and 3900 mature
links. Two candidate causes are excluded:

- **Redundancy.** `aa_prebundle_links_per_pair=1` drives the duplicate fraction
  0.63 -> exactly 0.000 and produces more unique pairs (2727 vs 2211), but `lcc`
  moves only 0.122 -> 0.133.
- **Link range.** Measured from the links that exist, capture geometry is
  **59 nm transverse / 104 nm axial, 100% antiparallel** — correct alpha-actinin
  physics that must not be lengthened.

The blocker is **barbed-end packing uniformity**. At A1600 3.2 s, per actin:
4.30 antiparallel partners available inside the real capture envelope, 2.90
actually linked, **31% with zero partners in reach**, **64% saturated at
`max_aa_bonds_per_actin=4`**. Bimodal — voids next to jammed pockets — where a
real Z-disc percolates because its lattice is uniform. A 2D random geometric
graph needs mean degree ~4.5 and we sit at 4.30, i.e. just under the transition.
Raising density makes it worse (A2400: 1.89 available, 46% with none), so the
lever is registration, not density.

Caveat: the ~4.5 threshold is the 2D random-geometric result and a Z-disc is a
finite-thickness slab, so read it as "near the transition", not exact. The
measurements that do not depend on it (31% with nothing in reach, 64% at the
cap) are the solid ones.

### Recommended next experiments

1. `max_aa_bonds_per_actin` 4 -> 6. One parameter, directly unbinds the 64%.
2. Anything improving axial registration of barbed ends into planes (addresses
   the 31%). Sherry's "align first, then register" idea targets this.
3. **Change the success criterion.** `perp_both` is a constant zero and cannot
   discriminate anything. Score on largest-component fraction, its transverse
   span, and the gap to random placement — those vary (z-span 8% -> 61%).

### `actin_turnover_clearance_ratio` — new parameter (the important one)

`_actin_renewal_position_is_clear` in `cpp/src/sarcomere.cpp` demanded the full
nominal hard core (`2*r_actin`, `r_actin+r_myosin`). The sterics are soft and
force-capped, so the equilibrium population sits **inside** that distance: only
**8.7%** of incumbent A800 actins and 23.7% of A1600 actins would pass the test
in their own current positions. Random-proposal acceptance was **0/1500** at
A800. Every renewal exhausted its 1000 attempts and silently did nothing — the
whole turnover axis would have been a null for an instrument reason.

Fix: `--actin_turnover_clearance_ratio` (default **0.80**, roughly the incumbent
p10-p25), plumbed through `main.cpp` -> `configure_actin_turnover` ->
`_actin_renewal_position_is_clear`, written to HDF5 attrs and required on resume
(legacy trajectories promote to 1.0). `--actin_turnover_max_placement_attempts`
raised 1000 -> **5000** because the mean accepted proposal takes ~410 attempts
and the observed max was 2346.

Validation: at an inflated k_turn=200 s^-1, 322 renewals vs 320 expected
(Poisson z=0.11), 0 failures, packing *improved* (median min a-a separation
0.0268 -> 0.0297), +13% wall clock at 200x the campaign's top rate, and
**k_turn=0 is bitwise identical** to the pre-change binary across actin/myosin
centres, directions and cb_status.

The ratio is a calibration of the acceptance rule to the model's own steady
state, not a physical parameter.

## 5. Traps and gotchas

**Partial trajectories look complete.** HDF5 files exist from the moment a run
starts. I twice reported n=3 arm statistics that silently mixed finished and
in-progress runs — the intermediate arm's apparent sd of 0.152 was that
artifact. **Always gate on `current_step >= nsteps`** before analysing:

```python
s = np.asarray(h["state/current_step"][:int(h["meta/committed_frames"][0])]).ravel()
complete = s[-1] >= 500000
```

**Seeds are bimodal.** Per-seed outcomes fall into two basins rather than
scattering about a mean (intermediate: 0.495 / 0.231 / 0.234; HCM M order 0.842
vs 0.545). n=3 cannot resolve arm ordering. Report P(good basin) with >=8-12
seeds if arm ordering matters. This is the same pattern as the arrest-rig
finding.

**The minus tip is the barbed end.** `c - 0.5*L*d`. The plus tip is the pointed
end. Getting this backwards makes linked-pair separation read as ~1.86 um (one
sarcomere period) instead of 104 nm, which is how I initially mis-diagnosed the
capture geometry. Sanity check: linked pairs should be **100% antiparallel**.

**Coordinates are centred on the origin**, x in [-Lx/2, +Lx/2] — not [0, Lx].
Rendering with a 0..L window shows one quadrant.

**A2400 "FAILED" jobs have complete data.** `40999593` (links1 kref1 kenc30) and
`41001095` (links3) both show FAILED with exit 1:0, but both produced 1001
frames at a full 5.00 s, and the profile line reports `calls=500000`. The logs
contain no error. Something in that older wrapper (most likely the health
monitor exiting nonzero under `set -e`) converts a successful run into a FAILED
job. **Unfixed** — it will keep tripping `afterok` dependencies. `40948948`
(links1) is genuinely partial at 3.30 s.

**Do not `pkill -f` over ssh** with a pattern that appears in your own command
line — it matches the remote shell running your command and kills the session.
Use `pkill -x <exe-name>`.

**Do not use `#` as a sed delimiter** when the pattern contains `#`
(`sed -i "s#^#SBATCH...#..."` prefixed every line of a script with garbage).
Prefer a small Python patch script with assertions on match counts.
