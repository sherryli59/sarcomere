# Handoff: does multi-head myosin binding align actin without an alignment potential?

Date: 2026-09-10
Supersedes the alignment-probe sections of `HANDOFF_20260830.md`.

The question this session converged on, after several detours (manuscript,
MyBP-C, lattice order — all set aside): **with `am_alignment_k = 0`, do multiple
myosin heads bound to the same actin generate actin–myosin alignment?**

Answer so far: **yes, and the operative variable is the AXIAL SPAN of the bound
heads, not their number. It aligns monotonically toward parallel (theta* ~ 0),
but weakly.** Details and the several ways I got it wrong first are below.

## 2. The central result

### The mechanism is axial span, not head count

Measured with the model's own instrument: for each bound head the code computes
`signed_drive = project_nematic_alignment_drive(u_m, u_a, tau_myosin x u_m)`
(`interaction.cpp:1179`), positive = rotates the pair toward alignment,
accumulated into `myosin/am_align_drive_{minus,plus}` with counts in
`am_align_head_count_*` and sign tallies in `am_align_{positive,negative}_count_*`.

**Crucially this excludes the `am_alignment_k` tether** — it is the head-spring
contribution alone in every arm. And it is a property of the force field at a
configuration, not a trajectory statistic, so it is immune to the escape,
selection and serial-correlation confounds that wrecked the earlier `dtheta`
analysis.

Observational split, production `motor_rest74_lateral_20260901`, `am_alignment_k=0`
arms of the probe, theta in [10,30) deg, drive/head in kT (kT = 4.114e-3 pN.um):

| k | span (nm) | crowns | episodes | drive/head |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 1 | 132 | **+1.02 +- 2.00** |
| 2 | **0** | 1 | 19 | **+1.62 +- 4.30** |
| 2 | 225 | 2 | 45 | **+13.45 +- 3.10** |
| 2 | 450 | 2 | 8 | **+30.96 +- 10.15** |
| 3 | 450 | 3 | 14 | **+31.47 +- 2.49** |

Two heads in the SAME crown do nothing — same count, zero span, the perfect
internal control. At fixed k=2 the drive scales with span (~0.065 kT/nm, a
force x lever-arm couple). At fixed span=450 adding a third head adds nothing.

Physical reason: same-crown pairs are 87 nm apart around the circumference and
rotate the filament about its own axis (**roll**); different-crown pairs rotate
it end-over-end (**tumble**). Only tumble changes theta. The model tracks these
as separate channels (`local_actin_torques` vs the axial `roll` term at
`sarcomere.cpp:6722`).

The confined-probe campaign **replicated this prospectively at k=2**:
span 0 -> -4.66 +- 3.10, span 225 -> **+14.72 +- 4.75** (vs +13.45 observationally).

### theta* is ~0 deg. The heads align monotonically, just weakly.

`span3` arm (`hph=9`, `am_alignment_k=0`), steady state = last half of each 2 s
run, 6 seeds pooled:

| initial tilt | mean theta | **median** | sd | % bound | % k>=2 | % span>0 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 deg | 21.65 | **12.86** | 23.2 | 54.4 | 46.2 | 41.3 |
| 30 deg | 22.48 | **8.97** | 21.3 | 63.3 | 59.1 | 57.3 |

The two tilts converge, so the rig is ergodic. But the MEAN is not the preferred
angle: the distribution is strongly right-skewed and its mode is the lowest bin.

Raw P(theta) decreases monotonically from 0 (counts 2694, 2240, 1910, 1011, 793,
776, 466, ... in 5 deg bins). That alone settles it, because the entropic prior
P0(theta) ~ sin(theta) VANISHES at theta=0 -- a histogram peaked where the free
measure goes to zero can only mean a potential minimised at 0. Dividing the
prior out, `U_eff/kT = -ln[P/sin(theta)]` has argmin **2 deg** in both tilts and
rises monotonically by 3-6 kT out to 30 deg.

The force agrees. Drive/head (kT) in the MINUS half (where `single_am_tilted`
puts the actin), by 5 deg bin from 0:
```
tilt 10:  +11.4  +16.4  +15.6  +4.4
tilt 30:  +12.9  +24.4
```
Positive down to 0-5 deg -- there is no angle at which the heads stop pulling.

Compare `alignON` (`am_alignment_k=120`): median theta 1.9 deg with 90% of
frames bound, vs `span3`'s median 12.9 deg with 54% bound. Heads alone align;
the explicit tether aligns far harder.

### The arms separate on P(latch), not on means — and that is the real result

Per-seed breakdown (12 runs per arm = 6 seeds x tilts 10 and 30, steady half):

| arm | runs that latch (>=83% of frames bound) | theta median, latched | theta median, escaped |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `span3` (hph=9, 3 crowns, k_rho=100) | **5 / 12** | **2.7 - 6.0 deg** | 13 - 68 deg |
| `span1` (hph=3, span identically 0) | **0 / 12** | — | 19 - 76 deg |
| `notrans` (hph=9, k_rho=0) | **0 / 12** | — | 8 - 78 deg |

Latched `span3` runs sit at perp 69-73 nm with theta median 2.7-6.0 deg, i.e.
essentially aligned and comparable to `alignON`. The distribution is bistable:
a run either latches into an engaged, aligned state or drifts off.

5/12 vs 0/12 vs 0/12 is a real separation (Fisher exact p ~ 0.04 for span3 vs
either control). But it is still CONFOUNDED: `span1` and `notrans` also cannot
stay engaged, so "no span -> no alignment" is not separable from "no engagement
-> no alignment" until the centre trap of section 6 exists.

**This is the bimodality trap from `sarcomere-arrest-rig-is-bistable` and I walked
straight into it** by reporting pooled means. Score these arms with P(latch).

**Caveat: the U_eff extraction fails its own null test.** Zero-bound-head frames
should recover a flat U_eff if the sin(theta) prior holds; they give argmin
12 deg (tilt 10) and 48 deg (tilt 30) with 4.4-6.6 kT spread. The prior is wrong
because a confined actin's accessible orientations are geometrically restricted
(80 nm closest approach to a 1.5 um rod in a 4x1x1 box) and the unbound frames
are a biased subsample, not free diffusion. So the DEPTH of U_eff is not
trustworthy; only the LOCATION of the minimum, which rests on the raw histogram
and the drive sign.

## 3. Corrections to earlier claims — read these before reusing any of it

1. **theta* is ~0 deg. I got this wrong three times.** First I reported 5.7-8.7 deg
   from a 20k-step smoke test, reading the last 50 frames of a trajectory whose
   sd is 22 deg -- a transient. Then I reported the 200k-step MEAN of 21.7-22.5
   deg as "a fixed point at 22 deg" -- but that is the mean of a right-skewed
   distribution whose median is 9-13 deg and whose mode and potential minimum
   are at ~0. **Never read a preferred angle off a mean**: the angle between two
   3D directors carries a sin(theta) measure with mean 57.3 deg under NO
   interaction, so any aligning potential minimised at 0 still yields a large mean.
   Use the raw histogram (peaked where sin(theta) vanishes) and the sign of the
   drive in the lowest bin.

1c. **The 22 deg was ALSO an average over a bimodal mixture.** Per seed, span3
   runs either latch (>=83% bound, theta median 2.7-6.0 deg) or escape (theta
   median 13-68 deg); 5 of 12 latch. Pooling them produced a meaningless
   intermediate. Always split by outcome first, then report within-basin
   statistics.

1b. **I also inferred a "preferred angle of 5-10 deg" from the `alignON` arm.**
   Wrong for a different reason: that arm is DRIVEN -- the explicit tether has
   already forced theta to ~2 deg, so the head springs are resisting an
   externally imposed rotation. The negative drive there is not evidence of an
   intrinsic preferred angle. Same explanation for production's -12 kT/head:
   the tether is simply much stiffer than the head couple. Only measure the
   intrinsic preference in an arm with `am_alignment_k=0`.

2. **The `dtheta` result (-0.247 to -0.277 deg/frame at >=2 heads) is dead.**
   `free_cap2`, `free_cap3` and `free_cap6` are **bitwise identical for seeds 1
   and 2** (verified on `actin/center` and `actin/direction` over all 1001
   frames); only seed 3 diverges, at frame 180. So "replicated across cap2/3/6"
   was one trajectory counted three times. **Whenever the swept knob is a cap or
   limit, check bit-identity first** — a cap that is never reached is a
   guaranteed null that manufactures duplicate runs looking like replicates.

3. **The production arm labels are inverted vs HCM biology.** HCM = destabilised
   SRX = MORE available heads (WT 42% SRX -> availability 0.58; R403Q 14% ->
   0.86; R663H 10% -> 0.90; Sci Adv 2020, title literally "increase the number
   of myosin heads available"). The campaign's `hcm` arm has availability 0.50
   and `fullon` has 1.00, so **`hcm` is wild type and `fullon` is HCM-like**.
   Every conclusion drawn from those arms currently reads backwards.

4. **My k>=3 span analysis in the confined campaign is invalid.** I pooled
   `am_align_drive_minus + _plus`, mixing the two myosin halves, so any span
   above the 675 nm bearing length is an opposite-half pair — a different
   geometry from the two-point-constraint argument. The observational production
   analysis grouped by (actin, myosin, **half**) and is fine. Redo the confined
   k>=3 rows per half.

5. **`am_head_transverse_k` is not a spacing spring and its floppiness is not a
   defect.** It is a head-RETENTION spring: coordinate rho (this head's
   myosin-surface anchor to the actin axis), rest 30 nm, one per bound head, the
   only radial term that makes torque. `am_tether_k` (3000 pN/um, rest 80 nm,
   pair centerline, pure force, no torque) is the spacing spring. Setting
   `k_rho=0` takes rho p95 from 48-59 nm to 89-97 nm and produces 1-3 forced
   ruptures per run vs zero. 1.4 pN is the right magnitude for that job.

6. **My attachment-rate figure was too high.** Production has
   `am_weak_binding_floor = 0.1` (the probe uses 1.0), so the rate is
   `100 x 0.692 (thin) x 0.776 (spatial) = 53.7 /s` against detachment
   160-200 /s. Detachment outruns attachment **~3.7:1**, not 2.6:1.

## 4. What limits multi-head binding in production

Per (actin, myosin, half) engagement, last 100 ms of the 1000 ms runs:

```
9 slots per half
  x 0.47  (capture reach, per actin)   -> 4.20 reachable
  x 0.326 (duty ratio)                 -> 1.37 bound
```
and per myosin half, taking the UNION over ~5 nearby actins:
```
9 slots x 0.88 (union reachability) -> 7.92 x 0.41 (duty) -> 3.25 bound
```

So **the head budget is not wasted** — 88% of slots can bind something and 60%
of halves have all 9 in play. The per-actin 4.2/9 is azimuthal division of
labour among the ~5 thin filaments each half serves, which is what the 40 deg
inter-crown twist is for. Crown span per actin is low because 3.25 bound heads
are spread over ~2.4 partner actins.

Measured nulls (do not bother tuning these): `am_cutoff` (median best-spoke
distance by crown rank is 2.4/8.8/15.2 nm against a 50 nm cutoff — all three
crowns already deep inside); the per-actin overlap capacity (8.07 available vs
1.37 used, 0.1% of engagements at capacity); `am_max_heads_per_actin_half` = 24;
actin sites (182 per actin, **1.16% occupied**); head availability (moves
<crowns> only 1.19 -> 1.28 across the whole arm ladder).

Real levers, ranked:
1. `am_k_weak_off` 200 -> 50 (the probe's value). Duty 0.33 -> ~0.6. Known side
   effect: more bound heads starves ACTN2.
2. `am_weak_binding_floor` 0.1 -> lower. Concentrates heads onto already-engaged
   partners at fixed budget — the only lever that raises crowns-per-actin
   without raising total drive.
3. `am_heads_per_half` 9 -> 36 takes <crowns> 1.07 -> 2.27 and levered heads
   12% -> 84%, but 4x the heads perturbs force balance and ACTN2 competition.
4. `am_binding_azimuth_half_width_deg` — AMBIGUOUS. Adds reachable slots per
   actin but also lets each slot choose among more actins, so the effect on
   crowns-per-actin may be negative. Downgraded from my earlier ranking.

## 5. Code changes this session

Branch `parallel-am-heads`, working tree (extensive uncommitted work left
untouched). Backups at `*.preconfine.bak`. Both new parameters default to inert
values and were verified **bitwise identical** against the pre-patch binary

### `am_tether_occupancy_floor` (+ `_reach`) — `sarcomere.cpp:6746`
Floors the bound-head occupancy multiplier `min(1, n_bound/am_tether_saturation_heads)`
on the **radial** pair tether only (the nematic angular tether keeps the
unmodified gate, so a confinement change can never be mistaken for an alignment
change). Reach-gated because `am_tether_force_max` defaults to 0 (unbounded
quadratic) and the neighbour list is effectively all-pairs. Default 0.0 = inert
and costs nothing (guarded).

Doubles as the MyBP-C constitutive-tether term if that work resumes: MyBP-C is
3 per 9 myosins in the C-zone, so floor = 3/6 = 0.5 against
`am_tether_saturation_heads=6` is the stoichiometric value, and MYBPC3
haploinsufficiency (24-40% protein loss) is ~0.33.

### `probe_tilt_deg` — `initialization.cpp` `single_am_tilted`
The hardcoded `constexpr double angle = 20.0 * M_PI / 180.0` is now a parameter.
Default 20.0 = inert.

### Freezing the myosin needs NO code
Crowding factors DIVIDE the mobility (`rod_mobility.h:77`) and the integrator
uses `sqrt(2 D dt)` / `sqrt(2 mu_tumble dt)` noise, so `myosin_crowding_*=1e9`
kills drift and noise together. Measured residual over 2 s: **0.28 nm and
0.003 deg**.

## 6. The confined probe rig, and its flaw

`confined_probe_20260910`, sbatch `cpp/submit_confined_probe_20260910.sbatch`,
source snapshot staged. 4 arms x 3 tilts x 6 seeds, 2 s at 0.5 ms/frame.

| arm | config | purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `span3` | `hph=9` -> 3 crowns, spans 0/225/450 | main |
| `span1` | `hph=3` -> `crown_count=(3+2)/3=1`, span identically 0 | span control |
| `notrans` | `hph=9`, `am_head_transverse_k=0` | is the couple the radial spring? |
| `alignON` | `hph=9`, `am_alignment_k=120` | positive control |

Confinement: `am_tether_k=100`, `am_tether_occupancy_floor=1.0`, reach 1.0 um.
Gave perp median 76 nm (target 80), 86% bound, 70% k>=2, force-cap events on
0.4% of steps in tuning.

### THE FLAW — fix this before running more
The pair tether constrains the **segment-segment closest approach**, not the
actin's centre. Two crossed rods can satisfy an 80 nm closest approach while
their centres sit 500 nm apart. So the confinement only works while enough
heads are bound to also pin orientation and axial position. Consequences:

* every `tilt=60` arm escaped (perp median 481-584 nm, 0.2-5% bound) — a 60 deg
  crossing angle satisfies the tether at one point with the centre far away;
* `span1` and `notrans` escaped even at tilt 10-30 (perp 400-490 nm, 5-13%
  bound) **for the same reason they were controls** — fewer or weaker heads.
  So the controls are confounded, not null. They did not fail to align; they
  failed to stay engaged, exactly like the old free probe.

Only `span3` at tilt 10 and 30 stayed properly engaged, and that is the only
place the 22 deg fixed point is established.

### SECOND FLAW: the reach exceeds half the box (my setup bug)

`am_tether_occupancy_floor_reach = 1.0 um` but the probe box is 4 x 1 x 1, so
the half-box in y and z is **0.5 um**. The reach exceeds half the box by 2x,
which violates the minimum-image assumption: **the confinement can pull the
actin toward a periodic GHOST of the myosin instead of the real one.** Valid
reach for this box is < 0.5 - 0.08 = 0.42 um.

Symptom, and how it was found: the drawn actin occasionally teleports. Measured
across all 24 tilt-30 runs, every apparent jump is **exactly one box vector**
(jump/box 0.927-1.000, or 1.33-1.36 when two axes flip together; one run jumped
3.942 um = Lx), and it occurs only when the actin sits at 0.95-1.000 of the
half-box, where two periodic images are equidistant and the min-image choice
flips. Ruled out: director sign flips (0 in all 24 runs) and genuine fast motion
(p99 true displacement per drawn frame is 0.09-0.21 um, an order of magnitude
below the jumps). Force-cap events are <1% of steps and are MORE frequent in
the non-jumping latched runs.

**What this invalidates:** the latched runs are fine (max separation 0.24-0.39
um, inside the valid regime), so theta ~ 2-6 deg in the latched state stands.
The escaped runs spent 3.6-35.5% of frames outside it with an ill-defined
confinement, which may have contributed to their escaping -- so **P(latch) =
5/12 vs 0/12 is NOT trustworthy and needs a rerun.** The span-dependent drive
result is unaffected (production 6x2x2 box, and it is a per-configuration force
measurement, not a trajectory statistic).

**Fix:** Ly = Lz = 3-4 um. A two-body system costs nothing extra, half-box
becomes 1.5-2 um beyond any observed excursion, and both the drawn jumps and the
ghost-confinement disappear. Keep any interaction reach comfortably under
half-box. Note the ORIGINAL free probe used the same 4 x 1 x 1 box with the actin
reaching 0.7-0.8 um, so it had the same degeneracy.

**Fix:** add a genuine 3-DOF harmonic trap on the actin's CENTRE toward a fixed
target in the frozen myosin's frame (`probe_confine_k`, target captured at
initialisation). Applied at the centre it still produces zero torque, but it
constrains all three translational degrees of freedom instead of one scalar.
That is what I originally sketched and wrongly replaced with the tether floor.

## 6e. THE ANSWER: the span couple cannot bootstrap. It amplifies, it does not initiate.

From `42776169` at ~17% of 5 s (three seeds per arm, so preliminary on absolute
values but the arm contrast is unambiguous).

**Not a binding deficit.** bound/actin is HIGHER in the sticky arm:
1.07/1.10/1.10 vs base 1.00/0.95/0.93.

**Bound pairs are not aligned.** theta between an actin and the myosin it is
BOUND to (median):

| arm | theta_bound | theta_near (close, unbound) | theta_random |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| base | **3.0 / 4.1 / 3.2** | 5.7 / 10.0 / 6.9 | 9.1 / 45.0 / 11.5 |
| sticky | **53.6 / 59.0 / 52.8** | 56.2 / 59.6 / 55.2 | 59.3 / 59.5 / 59.1 |

Sticky bound pairs are statistically isotropic (57.3 deg reference) and only
2-3 deg better than merely-nearby pairs. Base bound pairs are at 3-4 deg and
clearly tighter than nearby pairs.

**The head-spring drive is ~zero in sticky**: +1.6 / -3.8 / +0.2 kT per bound
head, against +16 to +55 kT in the confined 2-body probe. (Base is -9 to -15,
i.e. resisting its own tether, as established.)

**Cause — realized span COLLAPSED rather than doubled:**

| arm | mean span | P(>0) | P(450) | <crowns> |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| base | 50-66 nm | 0.174-0.216 | 0.050-0.075 | 1.19-1.24 |
| sticky | **6-11 nm** | 0.024-0.042 | 0.003-0.007 | **1.03-1.04** |

I predicted k_weak_off=50 would take mean span 77 -> ~163 nm. It FELL 6-7x.

**Why: span requires alignment and alignment requires span.** Span needs two
heads on one actin from different axial crowns, which needs the actin to run
ALONGSIDE the myosin far enough to reach more than one crown. At theta ~55 deg
the actin CROSSES the head-bearing zone and intersects it over a short axial
stretch, so only one crown reaches it (<crowns> = 1.04) and the couple is worth
nothing. A parallel actin runs beside the myosin for its whole length and
reaches all three crowns.

So the mechanism is a positive-feedback AMPLIFIER whose basin of attraction does
not include the disordered state. The base arm's explicit tether supplies the
seed: align the pair -> open multi-crown reach -> span 50-66 nm -> the couple
engages and reinforces.

**This retroactively explains the confined probe.** That rig supplied the seed by
construction: `single_am_tilted` starts at 10-30 deg with the actin held at
perp 80 nm, already nearly parallel and alongside the myosin. It is also why
every `tilt=60` arm failed there -- at large initial angles the mechanism cannot
start, exactly as here.

**S at frame 0 is 0.17-0.20 in BOTH arms**, so `myosin_x_noise` starts near
isotropic and this is failure to order, not decay from an ordered start. Base
builds 0.18 -> 0.94; sticky stays 0.10-0.16, flat from 12% to 19% of the run.

**Interpretation caveat that stands:** the sticky arm clips the actin force cap
on 63-65% of steps (base: 32-36%), and 16-20% of myosin steps (base: 0.1-0.25%).
That is the regime that invalidated the earlier theta result. So a null on the
*magnitude* of order is not cleanly interpretable -- but the span and theta_bound
measurements above are geometric facts about the sampled configurations and do
not depend on the integrator being faithful.

## 6f. IT IS POLYDOMAIN, NOT DISORDER — the gap is coarsening, not nucleation

S computed WITHIN ACTN2-connected actin clusters, about each cluster's own
director (largest eigenvalue of Q = <3/2 uu - I/2>), at 49-100% of 5 s:

| arm | S_global | clusters | largest | S within clusters>=5 | angle, top-2 directors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| base | 0.991-0.993 | 12-27 | 290-470 | **0.998** | **0.5-1.3 deg** |
| sticky | 0.168-0.328 | 22-73 | 116-299 | **0.954-0.991** | **21.6-64.9 deg** |

ACTN2 nucleates near-perfectly ordered domains of 100-300 actins in BOTH arms.
The only thing the tether adds is making the domains share one director.

**ACTN2 engagement is not the limit.** aa/actin in sticky rose 0.68-2.68 ->
**3.7-4.2**, versus 4.40 for base's lowest seed, and global order stayed ~0.1.
Raising `k_aa` would be a null; the earlier recommendation to do so was wrong.

**Mechanism:** a 1.5 um thick filament binds actins from several ACTN2 domains
simultaneously and cannot align to two directors at once, so with the tether on
each myosin is a long-range orientational relay that forces the domains to
agree. With it off, myosins still bridge domains but exert no torque on them.

**What to add, in order:**
1. **Myomesin / `k_mm` (M-band, myosin-to-myosin).** The natural domain-to-domain
   coupler, and it is BOTH set to 0 and dead code (computes forces, discards
   them). Also explains the floppy Z-disc hexagon finding. Highest value.
2. **Make titin orientational.** It links thick-filament ends to Z-discs
   (ACTN2 domains); as a point-to-point spring it transmits no torque.
3. NOT a better nucleator, and NOT the span couple (it cannot bootstrap, 6e).

**MEASUREMENT CORRECTION that affects earlier numbers in this file.** The S
values reported in 6e and above are referenced to the LAB x AXIS, which
conflates order with director orientation. base seed 20260815 read 0.383 then
0.680 x-referenced, but its eigenvalue S is **0.991** -- fully ordered about a
tilted director. So base was uniformly excellent and the apparent spread within
the base arm is largely that artifact. **Use the eigenvalue of Q whenever the
director is not pinned.** The sticky-arm conclusions are unaffected: its
eigenvalue S is 0.168-0.328, still far below base.
